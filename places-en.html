FEATURED / SENSŌ-JI

Sensō-ji 浅草寺

Tokyo · Asakusa   ·   Temple

浅

The place

A Buddhist temple in Asakusa. The approach from Kaminarimon through Nakamise offers a glimpse of the district’s long history. The neighbouring Asakusa Shrine is a separate place.

Tradition and meaning / Kannon devotion

Sensō-ji is dedicated to Shō Kannon, the bodhisattva of compassion. The temple describes Kannon as listening to the suffering of living beings. This describes a religious tradition, not a guaranteed outcome.

Getting there

Walk from Asakusa Station; exits vary by railway line, so check your route before setting out.

Before you visit

The temple calls its fortune slips Kannon Hyakusen. Follow the instructions on site when drawing one.

Plan your visit

Start at Kaminarimon, walk along Nakamise and continue to the main hall. Sensō-ji is a Buddhist temple: follow its own guidance rather than assuming shrine customs apply. Return the fortune stick to its container.

FEATURED / MEIJI JINGU

Meiji Jingu 明治神宮

Tokyo · Shibuya   ·   Shrine

明

The place

A wooded shrine precinct in central Tokyo. The walk from busy Harajuku into the tree-lined approach offers a striking change of pace.

Tradition and meaning / A shrine in the forest

Meiji Jingu enshrines Emperor Meiji and Empress Shōken. According to the shrine, its forest was created using trees donated when the shrine was established.

Getting there

Several stations, including Harajuku, provide access. The official guide notes that walking from the entrance to the main shrine takes additional time.

Before you visit

Allow time for the walk and wear comfortable shoes. Check opening times and current visitor guidance on the official site.

Plan your visit

From the Harajuku-side entrance, follow the wooded approach to the main shrine. The shrine suggests about an hour for a visit, with at least ten minutes from the entrance to the main buildings. Photography is prohibited in the worship area in front of the main shrine.

FEATURED / FUSHIMI INARI TAISHA

Fushimi Inari Taisha 伏見稲荷大社

Kyoto · Fushimi   ·   Shrine

伏

The place

Known for its vermilion torii-lined paths on Mount Inari, this shrine offers walks of different lengths beyond the main worship area.

Tradition and meaning / Harvest and work

Fushimi Inari Taisha describes its tradition of prayers for abundant harvests and prosperity in business. The mountain is also a place of worship, so observe local etiquette on the paths.

Getting there

From Kyoto Station, take the JR Nara Line to Inari, or walk from Keihan Fushimi-Inari Station. Check stopping patterns and service updates before travelling.

Before you visit

The mountain paths include slopes and steps. Choose a route that suits the weather and your fitness, and take care after dark.

Plan your visit

From JR Inari Station, visit the main shrine and continue toward Senbon Torii. You can turn back when you wish; JNTO estimates two to three hours for a full circuit of the mountain.

FEATURED / ISE JINGU

Ise Jingu 伊勢神宮

Mie · Ise   ·   Shrine

伊

The place

Ise Jingu comprises a number of shrines centred on Naiku and Geku. The two main precincts are separate, so allow time to travel between them.

Tradition and meaning / Jingu and tradition

Ise Jingu describes Naiku as dedicated to Amaterasu-Omikami and Geku to Toyo’uke-no-Omikami. Learning about each precinct provides context for a visit.

Getting there

The official access guide suggests visiting Geku before Naiku. Consult the official transport information when planning your route.

Before you visit

Respect photography restrictions and worship-area guidance, and allow enough time between the two precincts.

Plan your visit

Consider starting at Geku near Iseshi Station, then travelling to Naiku by bus or another service. The two sanctuaries are separate, so allow time for transport and waiting; check services and visiting hours before setting out.

FEATURED / IZUMO TAISHA

Izumo Taisha 出雲大社

Shimane · Izumo   ·   Shrine

出

The place

Known for enmusubi, or the tying of connections. Its official FAQ explains that these connections are not limited to romance or marriage, but include many kinds of human relationships.

Tradition and meaning / Connections

The shrine explains that enmusubi includes the many connections surrounding people, not only romance. This is a matter of faith and does not guarantee a particular result.

Getting there

Buses and other transport are available from JR Izumoshi Station. Check the official access guide and operator timetables.

Before you visit

Follow local worship and traffic guidance. Festivals and seasonal events can bring crowds.

Plan your visit

The shrine explains that connections extend beyond romance. Shrine etiquette can differ, so follow the instructions on site. Check the return bus timetable to Izumoshi Station before your visit.

FEATURED / DAZAIFU TENMANGU

Dazaifu Tenmangu 太宰府天満宮

Fukuoka · Dazaifu   ·   Shrine

太

The place

A shrine dedicated to Sugawara no Michizane, associated with learning and culture. Alongside worship, visitors can explore bridges, artworks and the surrounding streets.

Tradition and meaning / Learning and study

Dazaifu Tenmangu enshrines Sugawara no Michizane, revered in connection with learning and culture. Knowing this tradition adds context to a visit before an exam or new course of study.

Getting there

It is a walk from Nishitetsu Dazaifu Station. From Fukuoka, check the train or bus route that best suits your starting point.

Before you visit

Construction or events may change visitor routes. Check official guidance before setting out.

Plan your visit

From Nishitetsu Dazaifu Station, follow the approach and look for the bridges and ox statues before paying your respects. The shrine’s half-day itinerary explains the ox’s connection to Sugawara Michizane. Check official notices for any construction-related changes.

FEATURED / HOKKAIDO JINGU

Hokkaido Jingu 北海道神宮

Hokkaido · Sapporo   ·   Shrine

北

The place

A shrine in Sapporo’s Maruyama area, close to a park where the scenery changes with the seasons. Allow time for a gentle walk around the area.

Tradition and meaning / Northern history and nature

The shrine states that it enshrines three kami and Emperor Meiji. Its Maruyama setting offers a chance to consider local history and traditions alongside seasonal scenery.

Getting there

Walk from Maruyama-koen Subway Station; Hokkaido’s official tourism site gives an approximate walk of 15 minutes.

Before you visit

Opening hours can vary by season and around New Year. In snowy weather, wear footwear with good grip.

Plan your visit

Take Sapporo’s Tozai subway line to Maruyama-koen and combine the shrine with a walk around Maruyama. Hokkaido’s tourism guide estimates a 15-minute walk from the station; allow extra time for snow in winter.

FEATURED / ITSUKUSHIMA SHRINE

Itsukushima Shrine 嚴島神社

Hiroshima · Miyajima · Shrine

嚴

The place

A shrine on Miyajima known for its waterside corridors and great torii gate. The view changes with the tide.

Tradition and meaning

The shrine describes a tradition of revering deities associated with protection at sea. This is a religious belief, not a guaranteed outcome.

Getting there

From JR Miyajimaguchi Station, walk to the ferry terminal and cross to Miyajima. The shrine is within walking distance of the island pier.

Before you visit

Check ferry services, tide conditions and admission information before travelling.

Plan your visit

From Hiroshima Station, the JR Sanyo Line takes about 30 minutes to Miyajimaguchi; walk to the pier and take the roughly 10-minute ferry. The shrine’s route follows the corridors past the main sanctuary and stage. Check the tides for the view you hope to see.

FEATURED / NAMBA YASAKA SHRINE

Namba Yasaka Shrine 難波八阪神社

Osaka · Namba · Shrine

難

The place

A shrine in Namba known for its striking lion-head-shaped structure. It is a place of worship close to a residential neighbourhood.

Tradition and meaning

Osaka’s tourism guide describes traditions associated with warding off misfortune and prayers for success. Such beliefs do not guarantee outcomes.

Getting there

Walk from the Namba station area. The route varies depending on your rail line and exit, so check a map.

Before you visit

Check visiting and photography rules, and be considerate of worshippers and neighbours.

Plan your visit

This is an easy stop on a walk from the Namba station area: see the lion-head-shaped Shishiden and take time to pay your respects. Osaka’s tourism guide estimates about six minutes on foot, though the route depends on your line and exit.

FEATURED / YASAKA SHRINE

Yasaka Shrine 八坂神社

Kyoto · Gion · Shrine

八

The place

A shrine in Kyoto’s Gion district, next to Maruyama Park, with a close connection to the Gion Matsuri festival.

Tradition and meaning

Kyoto’s official travel guide explains the connection between the shrine’s history and Gion Matsuri. Explore that background as part of your visit.

Getting there

It is within walking distance of Shijo-Kawaramachi and can be combined with a walk through Gion or Higashiyama.

Before you visit

Festivals and events can bring crowds. Check local notices and traffic restrictions.

Plan your visit

Walk from Shijo-Kawaramachi to the shrine, then continue to neighbouring Maruyama Park. Kyoto’s travel guide estimates the walk from Shijo-Kawaramachi at about 13 minutes. Allow for crowds and restrictions during Gion Matsuri and other events.
