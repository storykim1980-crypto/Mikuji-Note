Hokkaido Jingu 北海道神宮

Hokkaido · Sapporo   ·   Shrine

北

The place

A shrine in Sapporo’s Maruyama area, close to a park where the scenery changes with the seasons. Allow time for a gentle walk around the area.

Tradition and meaning / Northern history and nature

The shrine states that it enshrines three kami and Emperor Meiji. Its Maruyama setting offers a chance to consider local history and traditions alongside seasonal scenery.

Getting there

Walk from Maruyama-koen Subway Station; Hokkaido’s official tourism site gives an approximate walk of 15 minutes.

Before you visit

Opening hours can vary by season and around New Year. In snowy weather, wear footwear with good grip.

Plan your visit

Take Sapporo’s Tozai subway line to Maruyama-koen and combine the shrine with a walk around Maruyama. Hokkaido’s tourism guide estimates a 15-minute walk from the station; allow extra time for snow in winter.

A closer look / Making the visit meaningful

The wooded Maruyama setting offers a transition from the city into a place of worship. Combine a visit with nearby Maruyama Park, allowing extra time for snow and ice in winter.

Chusonji Temple 中尊寺

Iwate · Hiraizumi · Temple

中

The place

A Buddhist temple reached by a wooded path in Hiraizumi. Its Golden Hall is part of the UNESCO-listed Hiraizumi heritage site.

Tradition and meaning

The 12th-century Konjikido offers a window into Pure Land Buddhist ideals and the craftsmanship of the period.

Getting there

From JR Hiraizumi Station, allow about ten minutes by bus or 30 minutes on foot. Check operating days and timetables in advance.

Before you visit

The approach is uphill; admission may apply to the Golden Hall and other areas. Follow opening and photography guidance.

Plan your visit

Visit the wooded approach and Golden Hall, then consider nearby Motsuji Temple if your schedule allows.

A closer look / Making the visit meaningful

The gilded Konjikido and its craftsmanship offer insight into the Pure Land aspirations of the Northern Fujiwara. Follow the uphill Tsukimizaka approach past the temple halls; check admission arrangements for Konjikido before visiting.

Miyagi · Shiogama · Shrine

鹽

The place

A historic hilltop shrine in the port city of Shiogama, known for its vermilion buildings, harbour views and spring blossoms.

Tradition and meaning

Local traditions connect the shrine with the sea and salt-making. This is a description of belief, not a promise of results.

Take the JR Senseki Line from Sendai to Hon-Shiogama Station, then walk approximately 15 minutes. Check the train timetable before setting out.

The main approach has 202 stone steps. Consider the weather and your mobility, and check alternative approaches in advance if needed.

Plan your visit

Visit as a day trip from Sendai and allow time for a walk around the port. Check your return train, particularly during the blossom season.

A closer look / Making the visit meaningful

Explore the grounds with the port town’s maritime and salt-making traditions in mind. The front approach has 202 steps; if stairs are difficult, check alternative approaches and transport in advance.

Risshakuji (Yamadera) 立石寺

Yamagata · Yamadera · Temple

立

The place

A mountainside temple complex known as Yamadera, with halls spread along a forested slope and views over the valley.

Tradition and meaning

The wooded stone approach is part of a long mountain-temple tradition. The poet Matsuo Basho also visited the site.

Getting there

The lower temple area is about seven minutes on foot from JR Yamadera Station. The upper precinct requires a separate climb.

Before you visit

There are roughly 1,000 steps to the upper area. Rain and snow can make them slippery; check conditions and wear suitable shoes.

Plan your visit

Walk from the station to the mountain gate and continue uphill only if conditions and your fitness allow. Check your return train in advance.

A closer look / Making the visit meaningful

Around 1,000 stone steps lead from the foot of the mountain toward the upper temple area. Pause along the way for the forest and views; check access conditions and wear sturdy shoes, especially in rain or snow.

Nikko Toshogu Shrine 日光東照宮

Tochigi · Nikko · Shrine

日

The place

A shrine dedicated to Tokugawa Ieyasu, known for its ornate carvings and Yomeimon Gate. It forms part of the UNESCO-listed Shrines and Temples of Nikko.

Tradition and meaning

Explore the history of Ieyasu’s enshrinement and Edo-period craftsmanship. Look for the famous carving of the three wise monkeys.

Getting there

Take the World Heritage sightseeing bus from JR Nikko or Tobu Nikko Station, about 15 minutes. Walking from the station takes around 45 minutes.

Before you visit

Admission may vary by building or area. The path to the inner shrine includes a long staircase; check opening conditions and your ability before climbing.

Plan your visit

See Yomeimon and the carvings, then, if time permits, visit nearby Rinnoji Temple and Futarasan Shrine. Separate admission conditions may apply.

A closer look / Making the visit meaningful

Look closely at the ornate Yomeimon gate and the details of the shrine buildings: the three monkeys are only one of many carved scenes. Allow time for the extensive grounds, stairs and areas with separate admission.

Sensō-ji 浅草寺

Tokyo · Asakusa   ·   Temple

浅

The place

A Buddhist temple in Asakusa. The approach from Kaminarimon through Nakamise offers a glimpse of the district’s long history. The neighbouring Asakusa Shrine is a separate place.

Tradition and meaning / Kannon devotion

Sensō-ji is dedicated to Shō Kannon, the bodhisattva of compassion. The temple describes Kannon as listening to the suffering of living beings. This describes a religious tradition, not a guaranteed outcome.

Getting there

Walk from Asakusa Station; exits vary by railway line, so check your route before setting out.

Before you visit

The temple calls its fortune slips Kannon Hyakusen. Follow the instructions on site when drawing one.

Plan your visit

Start at Kaminarimon, walk along Nakamise and continue to the main hall. Sensō-ji is a Buddhist temple: follow its own guidance rather than assuming shrine customs apply. Return the fortune stick to its container.

A closer look / Making the visit meaningful

The route from Kaminarimon through Nakamise to the main hall brings Asakusa’s commerce and Kannon devotion together. Incense and omikuji are optional; when it is busy, leave space for people moving through to worship.

The place

Getting there

Before you visit

Admission and access vary by area. The dark passage beneath the hall may not suit everyone; check accessibility before entering.

Plan your visit

Walk the approach from the station, or take a bus there and explore the shops on the way back. Check the temple’s current visitor information.

A closer look / Making the visit meaningful

The principal image is a hidden Buddha and is not ordinarily visible. Explore the main hall and approach as an introduction to the temple’s pilgrimage tradition; check availability and admission for the dark passage experience and other special areas.

Aoshima Shrine 青島神社

Miyazaki · Aoshima · Shrine

青

The place

A shrine on the small island of Aoshima, reached by bridge and surrounded by subtropical plants and coastal rock formations.

Tradition and meaning

The visit combines coastal scenery with local worship. The wave-like rocks around the island are known as the Devil’s Washboard. Miyazaki’s tourism guide also describes its association with prayers for relationships.

Getting there

From Aoshima Station on the JR Nichinan Line, walk toward the beach and cross the bridge to the island. Check train frequency and your return time.

Before you visit

Take care in strong wind, high waves and heat. Rocks can be hazardous depending on the tide and weather; follow local safety notices.

Plan your visit

Walk from the station across the bridge to the shrine, then explore the beach if time permits. Check the weather and tides in advance.

A closer look / Making the visit meaningful

Cross the bridge to an island where subtropical vegetation and wave-like coastal rocks surround the shrine. Wet rocks can be slippery: check the tide, weather and local safety guidance, and protect the vegetation.

Naminoue Shrine 波上宮

Okinawa · Naha · Shrine

波

The place

A shrine on a coastal bluff in Naha, associated with the history of the Ryukyu Kingdom and local prayers connected to the sea.

Tradition and meaning

JNTO describes traditions of praying for safe voyages and plentiful catches and harvests. These prayers express belief, not guaranteed outcomes.

Getting there

Walk from Asahibashi Station on the Yui Rail monorail. A JNTO guide estimates about 15 minutes; the time varies by exit and walking pace.

Before you visit

Prepare for strong sun and rain. Check opening and amulet-counter hours before visiting.

Plan your visit

Combine a monorail-and-walking trip from central Naha with a visit to the nearby shore. Follow local rules for beach access and photography.

A closer look / Making the visit meaningful

The shrine stands above Naha’s coast, where city and sea meet. Pair it with a walk to the beach; note that the road is visible from some viewpoints. Keep the worship space quiet and paths clear.
