FEATURED / HOKKAIDO JINGU

Hokkaido Jingu 北海道神宮

Hokkaido · Sapporo   ·   Shrine

北

The place

A shrine in Sapporo’s Maruyama area, close to a park where the scenery changes with the seasons. Allow time for a gentle walk around the area.

Tradition and meaning / Northern history and nature

The shrine states that it enshrines three kami and Emperor Meiji. Its Maruyama setting offers a chance to consider local history and traditions alongside seasonal scenery.

Getting there

Walk from Maruyama-koen Subway Station; Hokkaido’s official tourism site gives an approximate walk of 15 minutes.

Before you visit

Opening hours can vary by season and around New Year. In snowy weather, wear footwear with good grip.

Plan your visit

Take Sapporo’s Tozai subway line to Maruyama-koen and combine the shrine with a walk around Maruyama. Hokkaido’s tourism guide estimates a 15-minute walk from the station; allow extra time for snow in winter.

FEATURED / CHUSONJI TEMPLE

Chusonji Temple 中尊寺

Iwate · Hiraizumi · Temple

中

The place

A Buddhist temple reached by a wooded path in Hiraizumi. Its Golden Hall is part of the UNESCO-listed Hiraizumi heritage site.

Tradition and meaning

The 12th-century Konjikido offers a window into Pure Land Buddhist ideals and the craftsmanship of the period.

Getting there

From JR Hiraizumi Station, allow about ten minutes by bus or 30 minutes on foot. Check operating days and timetables in advance.

Before you visit

The approach is uphill; admission may apply to the Golden Hall and other areas. Follow opening and photography guidance.

Plan your visit

Visit the wooded approach and Golden Hall, then consider nearby Motsuji Temple if your schedule allows.

FEATURED / SHIOGAMA SHRINE

Shiogama Shrine 鹽竈神社

Miyagi · Shiogama · Shrine

鹽

The place

A historic hilltop shrine in the port city of Shiogama, known for its vermilion buildings, harbour views and spring blossoms.

Tradition and meaning

Local traditions connect the shrine with the sea and salt-making. This is a description of belief, not a promise of results.

Getting there

Take the JR Senseki Line from Sendai to Hon-Shiogama Station, then walk approximately 15 minutes. Check the train timetable before setting out.

Before you visit

The main approach has 202 stone steps. Consider the weather and your mobility, and check alternative approaches in advance if needed.

Plan your visit

Visit as a day trip from Sendai and allow time for a walk around the port. Check your return train, particularly during the blossom season.

Miyagi · Matsushima · Temple

瑞

The place

A Zen temple in Matsushima, known for its main hall rebuilt by Date Masamune and historic caves in the surrounding grounds.

As a temple associated with the Date family, its buildings and decoration offer insight into local Buddhist culture and history.

FEATURED / RISSHAKUJI TEMPLE (YAMADERA)

Risshakuji (Yamadera) 立石寺

Yamagata · Yamadera · Temple

立

The place

A mountainside temple complex known as Yamadera, with halls spread along a forested slope and views over the valley.

Tradition and meaning

The wooded stone approach is part of a long mountain-temple tradition. The poet Matsuo Basho also visited the site.

Getting there

The lower temple area is about seven minutes on foot from JR Yamadera Station. The upper precinct requires a separate climb.

Before you visit

There are roughly 1,000 steps to the upper area. Rain and snow can make them slippery; check conditions and wear suitable shoes.

Plan your visit

Walk from the station to the mountain gate and continue uphill only if conditions and your fitness allow. Check your return train in advance.

FEATURED / NIKKO TOSHOGU SHRINE

Nikko Toshogu Shrine 日光東照宮

Tochigi · Nikko · Shrine

日

The place

A shrine dedicated to Tokugawa Ieyasu, known for its ornate carvings and Yomeimon Gate. It forms part of the UNESCO-listed Shrines and Temples of Nikko.

Tradition and meaning

Explore the history of Ieyasu’s enshrinement and Edo-period craftsmanship. Look for the famous carving of the three wise monkeys.

Getting there

Take the World Heritage sightseeing bus from JR Nikko or Tobu Nikko Station, about 15 minutes. Walking from the station takes around 45 minutes.

Before you visit

Admission may vary by building or area. The path to the inner shrine includes a long staircase; check opening conditions and your ability before climbing.

Plan your visit

See Yomeimon and the carvings, then, if time permits, visit nearby Rinnoji Temple and Futarasan Shrine. Separate admission conditions may apply.

FEATURED / SENSŌ-JI

Sensō-ji 浅草寺

Tokyo · Asakusa   ·   Temple

浅

The place

A Buddhist temple in Asakusa. The approach from Kaminarimon through Nakamise offers a glimpse of the district’s long history. The neighbouring Asakusa Shrine is a separate place.

Tradition and meaning / Kannon devotion

Sensō-ji is dedicated to Shō Kannon, the bodhisattva of compassion. The temple describes Kannon as listening to the suffering of living beings. This describes a religious tradition, not a guaranteed outcome.

Getting there

Walk from Asakusa Station; exits vary by railway line, so check your route before setting out.

Before you visit

The temple calls its fortune slips Kannon Hyakusen. Follow the instructions on site when drawing one.

Plan your visit

Start at Kaminarimon, walk along Nakamise and continue to the main hall. Sensō-ji is a Buddhist temple: follow its own guidance rather than assuming shrine customs apply. Return the fortune stick to its container.

FEATURED / MEIJI JINGU

Meiji Jingu 明治神宮

Tokyo · Shibuya   ·   Shrine

明

The place

A wooded shrine precinct in central Tokyo. The walk from busy Harajuku into the tree-lined approach offers a striking change of pace.

Tradition and meaning / A shrine in the forest

Meiji Jingu enshrines Emperor Meiji and Empress Shōken. According to the shrine, its forest was created using trees donated when the shrine was established.

Getting there

Several stations, including Harajuku, provide access. The official guide notes that walking from the entrance to the main shrine takes additional time.

Before you visit

Allow time for the walk and wear comfortable shoes. Check opening times and current visitor guidance on the official site.

Plan your visit

From the Harajuku-side entrance, follow the wooded approach to the main shrine. The shrine suggests about an hour for a visit, with at least ten minutes from the entrance to the main buildings. Photography is prohibited in the worship area in front of the main shrine.

FEATURED / ZENKOJI TEMPLE

Zenkoji Temple 善光寺

Nagano · Nagano City · Temple

善

The place

A major Buddhist temple in Nagano, visited by pilgrims for centuries. Shops and restaurants line the approach from Nagano Station.

Tradition and meaning

Its principal image is the Ikko Sanzon Amida Nyorai, also called Zenkoji Nyorai. JNTO explains that it is kept hidden from public view.

Getting there

From Nagano Station, allow about 30 minutes on foot or 15 minutes by bus. Check the departure stop and current service.

Before you visit

Admission and access vary by area. The dark passage beneath the hall may not suit everyone; check accessibility before entering.

Plan your visit

Walk the approach from the station, or take a bus there and explore the shops on the way back. Check the temple’s current visitor information.

FEATURED / ATSUTA JINGU

Atsuta Jingu 熱田神宮

Aichi · Nagoya · Shrine

熱

The place

A shrine within easy rail reach of central Nagoya, set among spacious wooded grounds. JNTO introduces it as one of the city’s major shrines.

Tradition and meaning

It is traditionally associated with the sacred Kusanagi sword, which is not on public display. Check the treasure museum separately for current exhibits.

Getting there

Conditions and opening hours for the grounds and museum may differ. Check the official site for opening and closure information.

FEATURED / ISE JINGU

Ise Jingu 伊勢神宮

Mie · Ise   ·   Shrine

伊

The place

Ise Jingu comprises a number of shrines centred on Naiku and Geku. The two main precincts are separate, so allow time to travel between them.

Tradition and meaning / Jingu and tradition

Ise Jingu describes Naiku as dedicated to Amaterasu-Omikami and Geku to Toyo’uke-no-Omikami. Learning about each precinct provides context for a visit.

Getting there

The official access guide suggests visiting Geku before Naiku. Consult the official transport information when planning your route.

Before you visit

Respect photography restrictions and worship-area guidance, and allow enough time between the two precincts.

Plan your visit

Consider starting at Geku near Iseshi Station, then travelling to Naiku by bus or another service. The two sanctuaries are separate, so allow time for transport and waiting; check services and visiting hours before setting out.

FEATURED / HIYOSHI TAISHA

Shiga · Otsu · Shrine

日

The place

A shrine at the foot of Mount Hiei in Sakamoto, reachable by rail from Kyoto and known for the local Sanno Festival.

It is a centre of Hiyoshi, Hie and Sanno traditions, offering insight into the region’s relationship between mountains and worship.

Visit the shrine and walk through historic Sakamoto. Plan additional transport and time if you continue to Mount Hiei.

FEATURED / FUSHIMI INARI TAISHA

Fushimi Inari Taisha 伏見稲荷大社

Kyoto · Fushimi   ·   Shrine

伏

The place

Known for its vermilion torii-lined paths on Mount Inari, this shrine offers walks of different lengths beyond the main worship area.

Tradition and meaning / Harvest and work

Fushimi Inari Taisha describes its tradition of prayers for abundant harvests and prosperity in business. The mountain is also a place of worship, so observe local etiquette on the paths.

Getting there

From Kyoto Station, take the JR Nara Line to Inari, or walk from Keihan Fushimi-Inari Station. Check stopping patterns and service updates before travelling.

Before you visit

The mountain paths include slopes and steps. Choose a route that suits the weather and your fitness, and take care after dark.

Plan your visit

From JR Inari Station, visit the main shrine and continue toward Senbon Torii. You can turn back when you wish; JNTO estimates two to three hours for a full circuit of the mountain.

The place

A Buddhist temple on a hillside in eastern Kyoto, known for the wooden stage of its main hall and views over the city.

FEATURED / TODAIJI TEMPLE

Todaiji Temple 東大寺

Nara · Nara Park · Temple

東

The place

A Buddhist temple founded in the Nara period, famous for its Great Buddha Hall and monumental Buddha. It is part of the UNESCO-listed Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara.

Tradition and meaning

The Great Buddha provides a starting point for learning about Buddhist culture and the history of Nara. Remember that the temple remains a place of worship.

Getting there

Walk about 20 minutes from Kintetsu Nara Station, or take a city bus to the Todaiji Daibutsuden/Kasuga Taisha-mae stop and continue on foot.

Before you visit

Admission may be charged for the Great Buddha Hall and other areas. Nara Park’s deer are wild animals: keep a respectful distance and follow feeding guidance.

Plan your visit

Walk through Nandaimon Gate to the Great Buddha Hall, then explore Nara Park if time allows. Plan breaks for heat and crowds.
